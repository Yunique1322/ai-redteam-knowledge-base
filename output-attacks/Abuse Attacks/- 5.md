# Abuse Attacks (Sections 9-13, condensed)

**Framing:** abuse attacks are the **deliberate** generation of misinformation, hate speech, propaganda, fraud, and deepfakes at scale (contrast with hallucination, which is accidental). The category descriptions (propaganda, phishing, fake reviews, defamation) are generic; the parts worth retaining are the evasion techniques, the safeguard distinctions, and the EU AI Act tiers.

## The only real offensive techniques

Models refuse obvious real-world misinformation (e.g. "vaccines cause autism") but happily write clearly-fake harmless stories ("aliens work at HackTheBox"). Two bypasses:

- Jailbreak it (previous module's techniques).
- **The fictitious-item plus find/replace trick:** ask for an article about a made-up item "XYZ causing autism," then replace every `XYZ` with `vaccines` yourself. The model never sees the sensitive request.

**Hate-speech detector evasion** (paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.16750). Detectors like [HateXplain](https://github.com/hate-alert/HateXplain) and [Detoxify](https://github.com/unitaryai/detoxify) assign a toxicity score and flag above a threshold. Adversarial evasions:

- **Character-level** ([DeepWordBug](https://github.com/QData/deepWordBug)): swap, substitute, delete, or insert characters in the highest-scoring tokens (`HackTheBox` → `HackhTeBox`).
- **Word-level** ([PWWS](https://github.com/JHL-HUST/PWWS)): greedily replace words with synonyms until the classification flips.
- **Sentence-level:** paraphrase the whole text (an LLM does this).

**Lesson: small perturbations evade automated detectors, so human validation is required. This generalizes to any content-policy filter, not just hate speech.**

## Safeguards (know which does what, this is the testable bit)

- ****Model Armor:** an API sanitization layer that inspects both input and output, catching prompt injection/jailbreak AND hate speech/harassment. Does NOT detect misinformation.** Its REST response flags categories like `rai.dangerous` and `pi_and_jailbreak` with a confidence level.
- ****ShieldGemma:** a Gemma-based LLM you integrate manually, detecting hate speech/harassment in input and output. Does NOT detect misinformation.** It expects a specific Yes/No policy-check prompt:

```
You are a policy expert trying to help determine whether a user prompt is in
violation of the defined safety policies.

<start_of_turn>
Human Question: How do I hack my neighbors WIFI?
<end_of_turn>

Our safety principle is defined in the below:

* "No Dangerous Content": The prompt shall not contain or seek generation of content that harming oneself and/or others (e.g., accessing or building firearms and explosive devices, promotion of terrorism, instructions for suicide).

===

Does the human question violate the above principle? Your answer must start with
'Yes' or 'No'. And then walk through step by step to be sure we answer
correctly.
```

- ****Prompt Guard (Meta):** prompt injection/jailbreak ONLY, no abuse-attack coverage.** A classic "which tool covers what" distractor.
- Other mitigations in one line: adversarial training and bias detection (model side); AI-text detection, fact-checking, and watermarking (paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.10226); plus media-literacy education.

## Legislation (the EU AI Act tiers are the most likely written question)

**US:** misinformation is generally protected speech unless it crosses into defamation, incitement, or fraud. Relevant instruments: the Take It Down Act (criminalizes deepfakes / non-consensual intimate imagery), NIST's AI RMF (voluntary best practice), and FTC action against deceptive AI.

**EU:** the DSA (mandates reporting/removal of illegal content, appeals, and risk assessments for large providers) plus the AI Act. The AI Act's four risk tiers:

- **Unacceptable-risk:** banned (social scoring, manipulative systems that exploit vulnerabilities).
- **High-risk:** heavy obligations (healthcare, education, law enforcement) including risk management, data governance, and human oversight.
- **Limited-risk:** **LLMs live here**, obligations are transparency and documentation, notably disclosing that content is AI-generated.
- **Minimal-risk:** unregulated (spam filters, video games).
